Short answer: yes, if you'll build more than two sites. No, if you're building one and walking away. That's the entire decision, and I could stop typing right now. But I won't, because I'm physically incapable of passing up a chance to Divi-de some numbers for you. (That pun was on purpose. It does not get better from here.)

Every week someone lands in my inbox with the same question. Divi costs $89 a year, or $249 once, forever. Which one do you buy. The answer is a maths problem wearing a WordPress costume, and the maths is not even close. If you're brand new to all of this, start with [what Divi 5 actually is](/blog/what-is-divi-5-and-why-you-should-upgrade/) and come back.

> **TL;DR:** Buy Divi Lifetime ($249) if you'll build two or more sites and stick around past year three. Buy one year ($89) if you're testing the water or doing a single site. Lifetime pays for itself in about 2.8 years — $249 divided by $89. After that, it costs you nothing.

## The Math: When $249 Beats $89

Here's the table nobody wants to read but everyone should.

| Years | Yearly plan | Lifetime plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $89 | $249 |
| 2 | $178 | $249 |
| 3 | $267 | $249 |
| 4 | $356 | $249 |

Watch what happens between year two and year three. The lifetime plan stops costing money. The yearly plan does not. It keeps going, like a song in your head you can't remember the name of.

Time is the entire game here. If Shawshank taught us anything, it's that time is either a wall or an escape route, depending on which side you're digging from. Yearly people are tunnelling out one teaspoon of $89 at a time. Lifetime people paid once and walked straight through the front gate.

Divi has been around since 2013. Someone who bought lifetime back then — and yes, it was cheaper then too — has used it for thirteen years without paying again. The yearly people in that group have paid a number I won't say out loud, because it's rude to the yearly people.

The rule of thumb: **if you'll still be building in year three, lifetime wins. If you're not sure you'll reach year two, don't.**

## What You Actually Get (Both Plans Are the Same)

This is the bit that surprises people. The $89 plan and the $249 plan give you the exact same things: Divi, the Extra theme, Bloom, Monarch, Divi Dash, every template, support, and updates. Unlimited websites. The only difference is the clock.

$89 is a rental. $249 is a purchase.

If that sounds like an easy decision, hold your horses. There's a reason the rental exists, and it isn't that [Elegant Themes](https://www.elegantthemes.com/) loves you. If the maths already convinced you, [get Divi here](https://www.elegantthemes.com/affiliates/idevaffiliate.php?id=81533) and skip the lecture below.

## The Catch: "Lifetime" Has Changed Before

Here's my one strong opinion, backed by a number: **"lifetime" is not a promise, it's a price tag.**

Elegant Themes has already shown they'll tinker with the offer. It used to be cheaper. Then they raised it. There were stretches where new lifetime licenses went quiet while they nudged everyone toward yearly. Divi 5 launched with the Pro stuff — Divi AI, Divi Cloud, Divi Teams — billed separately, yearly, on top.

So what does $249 actually buy. Lifetime access to the theme and the builder. Plus a yearly bill for the AI add-ons, if you want them.

That isn't a scam. It's worth knowing before you hand over the card. The people who get upset are the ones who assumed "lifetime" covered every future feature, forever, no asterisks. It never did. There's always an asterisk. Usually a second, smaller asterisk behind the first one, doing a slow wave.

My take: the $249 lifetime license is still the best deal in the WordPress ecosystem if you build sites for a living. But you buy it for the theme and the builder. Not for a vague promise that nothing will ever change.

## Don't Buy the Lifetime License If…

I'd rather talk you out of $249 than take it and have you annoyed at me in a year.

- **You're building one site for yourself and will probably abandon it.** Pay $89 once. It's cheaper.
- **You're not sure you even like Divi yet.** Use the 30-day money-back guarantee to test the yearly plan first.
- **You just want a blog, not a page builder.** Divi is a bazooka. You need a pencil. Take a free theme and spend the $249 on coffee.
- **You think "lifetime" means nothing will ever, ever change.** See the section above. Nothing is forever, except maybe my ability to make a pun worse by explaining it.

I've built over 100 Divi sites and I still call the Divi Theme Builder "the big confusing screen with all the little boxes" half the time. (A client once corrected me. I told them it was the technical term. They didn't believe me.)

## Straight Answers

**Is Divi lifetime still available in 2026?**
Yes, at $249 for the standard lifetime plan. Pro add-ons (Divi AI, Cloud, Teams) are separate yearly costs.

**Does Divi have a free version?**
No. There's a demo and a 30-day money-back guarantee, but no permanent free tier.

**Is the lifetime license really unlimited sites?**
Yes. Both the yearly and lifetime plans allow unlimited websites on one account.

**What's the break-even point?**
About 2.8 years. $249 divided by $89. If you use Divi past the start of year three, lifetime was cheaper.

**Can I upgrade from yearly to lifetime later?**
Elegant Themes has offered upgrade paths before, but pricing and availability change. Don't count on the terms being identical in two years.

**Is Divi 5 included?**
Yes. Divi 5 is included with the membership, whichever plan you pick.
