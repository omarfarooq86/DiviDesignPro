No. AI is not going to kill WordPress. It's going to move in, rearrange the furniture, and occasionally leave the milk out. That's a very different thing — and the difference is the entire point of this post.

Every few years the internet finds a new WordPress killer. Page builders were going to kill it. Wix was going to kill it. Headless was going to kill it. WordPress is the horror-movie villain of the CMS world: everyone keeps announcing the funeral, and it keeps sitting up on the gurney. If you're new to all this, [here's what Divi 5 actually is](/blog/what-is-divi-5-and-why-you-should-upgrade/) before we get into who's supposedly committing the murder.

> **TL;DR:** AI isn't replacing WordPress — it's being absorbed into it. WordPress still runs ~41% of the web in 2026. The real story isn't a death, it's a reshuffle: AI is landing in the core, in page builders like Divi, and in maintenance tools. The danger was never AI building your site. It's nobody maintaining the site AI built.

## The Short Answer: AI Isn't Killing WordPress, It's Assimilating It

Think of AI less like a competitor and more like electricity. Nobody says "electricity killed the factory." The factory plugged in and kept running — just faster, with better lighting. That's what's happening here. AI is becoming a utility that WordPress plugs into, not a rival platform standing across the ring.

WordPress core already treats AI as plumbing. There's now an Abilities API that lets plugins and themes register machine-readable functions an AI agent can actually discover and use. Translation: WordPress is building the sockets so the electricity has somewhere to go.

So the honest answer to "is AI going to kill WordPress" is no. But — and this is the bit the clickbait headlines skip — that doesn't mean nothing changes. Plenty changes.

## Why the "WordPress Killer" Headline Keeps Coming Back

Ask around and you'll land in one of three camps.

- **Camp one:** AI ends WordPress. Full stop. Type a sentence, get a website, nobody needs a CMS.
- **Camp two:** We've heard this before. Remember when [page builders](/blog/divi-5-vs-elementor-honest-comparison/) were the end of WordPress? It ate them and got stronger.
- **Camp three:** WordPress wins *because* of AI, by absorbing it faster than anyone expected.

The truth is a boring blend of camps two and three, which is exactly why nobody writes headlines about it. "Platform Integrates New Technology In A Measured Way" has never once gone viral.

Here's a number for the doom camp to chew on: WordPress sat at roughly **41.2% of the web** in August 2026, [per W3Techs](https://w3techs.com/technologies/details/cm-wordpress). Yes, that's down from a peak near 43.6% in early 2025. But the slide flattened over the summer — and part of that "loss" is a measurement quirk, because an AI-generated static site often gets counted as "no CMS" rather than as a WordPress defector. Losing a couple of points over eighteen months isn't a murder. It's a platform the size of a continent shifting slightly in its chair.

## Where AI Actually Shows Up in WordPress

This is the useful part. AI isn't one thing bolted to one place. It's turning up at every layer:

- **Content.** Draft copy, alt text, meta descriptions, translations.
- **The core.** The Abilities API, so agents can operate a site programmatically.
- **Design and page building.** This is where Divi lives — [Divi AI](https://www.elegantthemes.com/affiliates/idevaffiliate.php?id=81533) generates layouts, copy, and images right inside the builder, and Elementor has shipped its own assistant too.
- **Maintenance.** The unglamorous stuff — updates, uptime, security scans — increasingly comes with an AI co-pilot.

For a Divi user, that fourth bullet is quietly the best one. The tedious 20% of every project — the resizing, the alt text, the "make this section but blue" — is exactly what AI is good at. It isn't coming for your creativity. It's coming for your Tuesday-afternoon busywork, and honestly, it can have it.

## The Reshuffle Is Real (Even If the Death Isn't)

Here's what *is* genuinely shifting. New AI-native builders are showing up, and some are serious. Cloudflare shipped one called EmDash — built on Astro, with an AI server baked in — and there'll be more Astro-based challengers. Meanwhile the incumbents aren't sitting still: Divi and Elementor are racing to bolt on agentic features, and a pack of newcomers (Builderius, Novamira, ZipWP) are all sprinting for the same lane.

So the board is being rearranged. But rearranged is not flipped over. WordPress had a head start most people forget — Matt Mullenweg was on stage back in 2022 telling the community to "learn AI deeply," back when most of us still thought a chatbot was just a customer-service annoyance. The "WordPress is playing catch-up" narrative is simply wrong. It's been running this race for years.

## My Take: The Danger Isn't AI. It's Vibe-Coding a Business Into the Ground.

Here's my one strong opinion, and I'll happily die on this hill: **a live URL is not the same as a living business.**

AI is fantastic at the generation — the thrilling first 80%, where a whole site appears out of a paragraph of text. It is terrible at the boring 20% that decides whether that site still exists in a year. Who patches it? Who fixes it when a plugin update white-screens the homepage? Who notices the contact form quietly stopped emailing three weeks ago?

I've built over 100 Divi sites, and I promise you the hard part was never making something appear on screen. The hard part is making it *keep working* after everyone's stopped paying attention. You can absolutely vibe-code a project straight into the ground: a gorgeous AI-spun homepage sitting on free hosting that nobody's actually driving. It's a beautiful car with no one in the seat.

That's why "an AI built it in an afternoon" isn't the flex people think it is. The afternoon was never the expensive part.

## What This Means for Divi Users (The Practical Bit)

So what do you actually *do* with all this? Three things.

1. **Use AI for the draft, not the decision.** Let Divi AI rough out a layout or a first-pass hero. Then bring the taste. The tool proposes; you dispose. If you're going the AI-design route, here's [how to turn an AI-generated design into a real Divi 5 layout](/blog/how-to-convert-ai-generated-designs-to-divi-5-layouts/) without it falling apart on mobile.
2. **Sell the maintenance, not just the build.** The build is getting commoditized. The "keep it alive" part is getting *more* valuable, not less. That's the moat now.
3. **Stay on maintained ground.** Whatever you build on — Divi, a block theme, whatever — pick tools with a company and a roadmap behind them. AI moves fast; you want a floor under it that isn't going anywhere.

## Straight Answers

**Is AI going to kill WordPress?**
No. WordPress still powers about 41% of the web in 2026. AI is being absorbed into WordPress — the core, the page builders, the maintenance tools — not replacing it.

**Will AI replace WordPress developers?**
It replaces the boring parts, not the job. Someone still has to make the decisions, fix what AI breaks, and keep the site alive after launch.

**Can AI build a complete WordPress site on its own?**
It can build something that loads. Whether it ranks, converts, and survives its first plugin update is the real test.

**What is Divi AI?**
Elegant Themes' built-in assistant — it generates copy, images, and layouts inside the Divi builder. It's a separate add-on to the Divi membership.

**Is WordPress still worth learning in 2026?**
Yes. Every AI tool worth using still needs someone who understands how a site actually works.

**Should I move a client off WordPress to an AI builder?**
Not without asking who maintains it in a year. If longevity matters, build on maintained ground.

## The Last Word

AI and WordPress are both too big to cancel each other out, so they're going to do the sensible thing and interface. The page builders that made WordPress dominant the first time — Divi very much included — might be the thing that gives it a second life.

So no, put the eulogy away. WordPress isn't dying. It's just got a very fast, very confident new housemate who's brilliant at chores and occasionally reorganises your kitchen without asking. Learn to work with it, keep charging for the part it can't do, and you'll be fine.
